* Status and intent

This is a non-normative engineering note accompanying Gessoplan R5.1.
It records a promising future multi-node implementation of Gesso Live and
identifies the architectural decisions we should preserve while completing
single-node Gesso. It is not an instruction to implement multi-node support
now, add Valkey dependencies, expand current admission profiles, or interrupt
the current authoritative-operation refinement work.

The implementation observations use the v2756 source snapshot
(=03e5e689e3cf=). Revision v2757 added the related
[[file:gesso-provable-operation-portfolio.org][provable operation portfolio]] design note. Neither document establishes
a new proof result or changes canonical R5.1.

The proposal originates in the November 25, 2026 design
/Hierarchical SSE Invalidation & Multi-Node Routing Architecture/ and the
subsequent discussion of its relationship to Gesso. In particular, the
proposal uses prefix-expanded Valkey Pub/Sub subscriptions instead of
standalone firehose routers and probabilistic vacuum filters. The
discussion establishes a key design principle: missed broker notifications
are fundamentally another instance of an observer failing to acquire fresh
authoritative state, rather than a failure to perform a business mutation.

The plan is conditional on qualification of the eventual provider,
transport and installed deployment. Its performance goals are hypotheses,
not measured capacities.

* The design principle: one semantic system, two deployment topologies

Single-node and multi-node Gesso should implement the same application
semantics. Distribution changes how an admitted operation is hosted, how
a relevant observation becomes invalidated, and how an observer acquires
a sufficient authoritative basis. It must not change the meaning of the
operation, the authorization decision, the business invariant, or the
client's authoritative installation rule.

The following distinctions are central:

- A committed authoritative operation changes durable state.
- A publication intent records an obligation to notify about the change,
  where the admitted operation requires publication.
- An invalidation is a notification that an observation may be stale,
  together with any applicable progression requirement.
- Transport acceptance is evidence of acceptance at a particular boundary;
  it is not evidence that all recipients obtained the notification.
- Acquisition obtains an authoritative observation at a sufficient basis.
- Installation accepts that observation into the intended client owner,
  subject to identity, progression, continuity and presentation policies.

An invalidation must not itself incorporate the business operation or
contain an indispensable incremental state mutation. If it did, dropping
the message would create a fundamentally different recovery problem.

The same declaration of an operation, invariant, publication scope and
client policy should work whether the serving application consists of one
process or many. Deployment profiles may differ in their qualified
assumptions and resource limits, but not silently change that declaration.

* Missed notifications and observer recovery

** One semantic failure class

Consider a client whose installed authoritative observation is at basis B0.
The database subsequently incorporates relevant changes up to basis B1.

The client may fail to observe the changes because:

- It is offline, closes its SSE stream, or never establishes an interest.
- Its serving node loses the Valkey subscription connection.
- The originating process fails before attempting notification.
- A subscriber, local dispatch queue or SSE stream drops an invalidation.
- The client receives an invalidation but its acquisition request fails.
- The query returns an insufficient basis or installation is rejected.

These are different operational failures but share a semantic consequence:
the observer has not acquired and installed an adequate authoritative
observation. They do not by themselves invalidate a correctly committed
business transition.

Recovery need not reproduce every missed message. If a qualified
authoritative frontier F covers B1 and any still-pending requirements,
then reacquisition at or beyond F can replace all those missed refresh
requirements. This is safe because a refresh notification is not a
business effect to be executed once for every message.

For advisory presentation, attaining freshness may mean making the
authoritative update available under the declared policy, not forcibly
replacing a view that the user is entitled to retain. Distinguish the
installed observation, visible presentation, and known pending update.

** The remaining difference: knowledge of failure

A browser may notice its own disconnection. It may not know that the
serving web node lost its upstream notification source while the SSE
connection stayed open. Likewise, a publisher failure may occur without
any subscriber disconnect.

The system must therefore represent notification continuity as uncertain
when a transport epoch changes or a gap is suspected. When continuity
cannot be established, conservatively reacquire. Reconnection, detected
queue overflow, lost subscription acknowledgment and uncertain worker
outcome are all reasons to initiate the applicable reconciliation.

Some losses are silent. Periodic reconciliation or another qualified
gap-detection mechanism is required if the requested freshness property
covers such failures. An ephemeral broker cannot establish absence of
loss merely by continuing to accept commands. A heartbeat demonstrating
connection health does not demonstrate that every prior message arrived.

** Safety and liveness are different

The primary safety obligation is that every admitted committed operation
preserves the declared business invariants. Observation lag does not
authorize an otherwise forbidden command; authoritative eligibility must
be re-established at the protected execution boundary.

For the live observation path, safety includes not installing a response
for the wrong subject, owner, or authorization context, and not regressing
an authoritative basis without an explicitly permitted semantic transition.

A useful liveness target for an automatically refreshing observation is:

#+begin_example
If a relevant change commits at basis B, and an interested observer
continues or resumes participating, then under the qualified availability,
fairness and recovery assumptions the observer eventually installs an
authorized observation whose basis satisfies B.
#+end_example

This does not assert bounded delay during a partition, exactly-once SSE
delivery, or visibility of every intermediate state. Where the application
declares advisory refresh, eventual availability of the update and
installation following a permitted refresh are distinct properties.

State explicitly the necessary assumptions about observer participation,
stable interests, available authority, fair dispatch/recovery, and successful
permitted installation. Continuous mutation must not cause indefinite
starvation of an already pending refresh obligation.

* Eventual implementation architecture

** Topology

Each web node hosts the qualified application, Live dispatch and local
interest index, HTTP and SSE connections, and access to the same logical
XTDB authority. Kafka and object storage are proposed backing components
for the future XTDB deployment. The exact XTDB indexing and replication
topology is a provider qualification question, not a Gesso semantic premise.

Valkey provides ephemeral macro-routing between application nodes. It
does not own business state, authorization, authoritative progression,
or the durable publication record. HAProxy may route requests by tenant
or store to improve locality. A qualified deployment must remain correct
when requests do not follow that affinity.

#+begin_example
                 authoritative operation
                           |
          atomic durable state + publication intent
                           |
                  recoverable outbox worker
                           |
              +------------+-------------+
              |                          |
       optional local fast path    Valkey Pub/Sub
              |                          |
              +------------+-------------+
                           |
                interested web nodes
                           |
               local indexed SSE fanout
                           |
              progression-aware acquisition
                           |
                authorized installation
#+end_example

The durable recovery path is the authoritative database and the applicable
publication/reconciliation machinery, not a requirement to durably retain
every broker notification.

** Semantic invalidation before physical routing

A writer declares a primary change. Application invalidation expansion
determines the affected scopes. The routing adapter distributes those
already determined invalidations.

For example, a change to a request can affect a store queue, request detail,
helper assignment list, and organizational view. These relationships
need not form a single tree. Several independently rooted scopes can be
affected by one operation.

Do not confuse a hierarchical route with a business dependency. A
subscription to a parent path does not automatically match a child
invalidation under downward-prefix semantics. If a board depends on the
child, the semantic expansion must include a scope covering the board.

In the current implementation,
[[file:../src/gesso/live/invalidation.clj][gesso.live.invalidation]]
already separates change expansion from database reads, SSE and the source.
It carries the primary progression requirement through expansion and
does not allow application rules to invent or replace that requirement.
Preserve this separation.

In particular, do not make publication of an affected scope conditional
on whether a local process currently knows of a subscriber. Live interest
tables are delivery optimizations. A relevant remote observer may exist,
or an acquisition may be in progress. The operation's declared publication
obligation must not depend on the originating JVM's view of demand.

** Prefix-expanded Valkey subscriptions

The proposed hierarchy is downward, segment-aware invalidation. Let p
be an invalidated path and q an interest path. They match precisely when
p is an ancestor of q, including equality.

#+begin_example
matches(p, q) iff p belongs to prefixes(q)

Interest:     [:store 419 :queue :item 7]
Subscription: all nonempty semantic ancestor paths
Invalidation: [:store 419 :queue]
Result:       matches the interest
#+end_example

The representation is illustrative, not an existing Gesso API. It is
important that paths have typed, unambiguous segment boundaries.

For every locally registered interest, derive its ancestor channel set.
Maintain desired per-channel reference counts. A web node needs a broker
subscription only if at least one local interest contributes that channel.
Publish each invalidation to its own encoded path. Valkey exact-channel
matching selects the web nodes with matching interests, and the receiving
nodes perform exact local fanout.

For this hierarchy the transformation is exact, not probabilistic. It
avoids pattern subscriptions and avoids synchronization of full routing
tables between all web nodes. It does not imply constant-time delivery:
broker publication work grows with receiving subscriber connections, and
local fanout grows with the relevant subtree and matching consumers.

Existing exact topic/id matching remains a valid semantic routing mode.
Do not prematurely replace the current topic/id representation with
arbitrary dotted strings. A future hierarchy requires a qualified scope
representation and an injective, deployment-namespaced channel encoding.
Consider type distinctions, escaping, segment boundaries, maximum depth,
channel length, and unsupported scopes. A string prefix such as store_41
must not accidentally match store_419.

The initial distributed adapter can even route exact topic/id interests
before hierarchical support is introduced. Hierarchical routing is an
independent optimization and expressive extension, not a prerequisite
for the basic multi-node consistency protocol.

** Local interest index

[[file:../src/gesso/live/source.clj][gesso.live.source]] presently uses an
in-process indexed source with exact topic/id routing and bounded consumer
resources. A future hierarchical implementation could use a root-sharded
persistent trie, with each reader traversing an immutable snapshot.
Independent roots reduce unnecessary update contention.

A concurrent sorted range index is another candidate, but raw character
prefix scans do not implement segment semantics. Weakly consistent
iteration may miss concurrent registrations. Idempotency permits extra
notifications, not undetected omission of relevant observers; acquisition
and recovery must cover registration races.

Maintain desired, broker-confirmed and pending subscription state
separately. A stale unsubscribe must not defeat a newer registration.
Serialize or reconcile commands against the latest desired state and
connection epoch. Avoid inventing a global lock or global routing table
as a correctness dependency.

At a later scale, Valkey sharded Pub/Sub can partition channel routing
without restoring the dedicated modulo-router subsystem. The initial
implementation should favor simplicity; choose and qualify the JVM client,
subscription acknowledgments, reconnect semantics, and cluster behavior
at implementation time.

* Acquisition, progression and reconciliation

** Acquisition is a semantic barrier, not a TCP event

A subscription is not safely acquired simply because an SSE socket is
open or a broker subscribe command has been enqueued.

An illustrative future acquisition protocol is:

1. Install the local interest in an acquiring generation so incoming
   matching notifications can be accumulated.
2. Derive its required broker channels and establish their subscription
   state for the current transport epoch.
3. Confirm subscription establishment.
4. Obtain an authoritative frontier covering the required prior history.
5. Acquire and qualify the observation at or beyond that frontier.
6. Compose and reconcile requirements received while acquisition was
   in progress, preserving any unsatisfied requirement.
7. Activate the interest without losing a notification racing with the
   transition to active state.

A committed change before the acquisition frontier must be covered by
the read. A subsequent relevant change must be observed or recovered by
the qualified reconciliation path. The handover from acquisition to
normal dispatch must have no unaccounted gap.

The frontier must come from a qualified source covering the shared
authority. The latest transaction already indexed by a lagging local
replica is not necessarily sufficient. A request may be served by a
different node than the one hosting SSE.

On reconnect, establish a new transport epoch, restore desired interests
and repeat the applicable acquisition/reconciliation barrier. It is
permissible to keep browsers' SSE sockets open while marking their
upstream notification continuity uncertain and issuing a resync.
Do not conflate upstream reconnection with a business-state transition.

** Preserve the existing progression vocabulary

[[file:../src/gesso/live/progression.cljc][gesso.live.progression]]
intentionally treats bases as opaque values. Generic composition retains
the set of distinct requirements, rather than inferring ordering from
arrival order, timestamps or an arbitrary token representation. Reduction
requires authority-specific progression evidence.

This is already a good foundation for multiple publishers and out-of-order
delivery. In particular, an older notification must not overwrite a
stronger pending requirement, and a stale response must not satisfy it.

The eventual broker envelope should carry the existing semantic
progression requirement using a qualified versioned encoding, rather than
make an XTDB-specific await token the generic Live API. The protocol
would also need deployment/routing-domain identity, publication identity,
scope and the applicable transport version. These fields are proposed,
not present wire requirements.

[[file:../src/gesso/live/consistency/xtdb.clj][gesso.live.consistency.xtdb]]
owns provider-specific basis representation and comparison, including
database compatibility, strongest-required-basis, query options and
request-frontier binding. Retain the distinction between a transaction
catch-up requirement, a selected snapshot and the actual observed basis.
Waiting for an index position alone is not evidence that an arbitrary
query or rendered response used the required snapshot.

XTDB await tokens are a plausible implementation mechanism for
cross-node read-after-write. Validate the exact API and semantics for the
provider version and installed topology, including timeout, unavailable
authority and cross-node token compatibility. The current dependency is
XTDB 2.2.0-beta1. Do not assume documentation for a different version
establishes compatibility.

If a required basis is incompatible, untrusted, or cannot be satisfied,
the adapter must report an appropriate failure or pending outcome rather
than render a falsely qualified observation. Client-supplied progression
data does not itself authenticate an authority or authorize access.

** Recovery granularity and cost

The initial recovery implementation can conservatively invalidate all
affected local interests after subscription restoration, at a qualified
frontier. This is not an instruction to reconstruct every missed
invalidation. A full resync is a safe fallback when the loss interval
cannot be identified, subject to appropriate bounded scheduling.

Later, use durable publication/history coverage or authoritative scope
frontiers to narrow reconciliation. Do not assume a presently implemented
outbox history scheduler is already a distributed replay protocol.
History retention, cursor advancement, completeness, lease ownership,
and behavior when a cursor predates retained history need qualification.

A transport gap can be handled at the web-node level; there is no need
for a database row per client per notification. Browser acquisition
recovery remains necessary even with reliable broker delivery.

* Durable publication and dispatch acknowledgment

** Keep the committed intent

The fact that missed notifications are recoverable does not mean the
normal notification path should be allowed to lose committed intents
routinely. A durable outbox closes the ordinary commit-to-publish crash
window without requiring distributed atomic commit with Valkey.

In the current implementation, publication intent is part of the
authoritative operation's mapped durable effects where declared.
[[file:../src/gesso/live/outbox/model_publication.clj][Model publication]],
[[file:../src/gesso/live/outbox/acquisition.clj][lease acquisition]],
[[file:../src/gesso/live/outbox/delivery.clj][delivery]],
[[file:../src/gesso/live/outbox/acknowledgment.clj][acknowledgment]] and
[[file:../src/gesso/live/outbox/reconciliation.clj][reconciliation]]
provide a substantial single-node foundation.

The delivery adapter uses a successful XTDB lease-acquisition receipt
as a conservative progression basis, checks the currently leased intent
and source identity, and requires local dispatch acceptance before
recording the guarded acknowledgment. The acknowledgment checks the
lease fence and receipt shape. Its own contract explicitly does not
establish adapter authenticity, SSE transmission or browser use.

In a future distributed deployment, a worker can discover pending intents
and reclaim an expired lease under a qualified shared database protocol.
If it publishes and crashes before acknowledging, the publication may be
repeated. The notification path must tolerate that duplication; the
business operation must not be repeated as a side effect of dispatch.

** Keep acknowledgment scoped to the adapter

The existing delivery receipt's =:gesso.live/synchronous-source=
classification describes a local acceptance boundary. It is legitimate
for its currently qualified scope. It must not silently become evidence
that a distributed notification was accepted.

A future distributed publication adapter must have its own explicit
acceptance contract, appropriate trusted provenance and lease-fenced
acknowledgment. Broker command acceptance is a plausible boundary: it
means the adapter successfully submitted the refresh publication to the
qualified broker, not that every intended node or browser received it.

A zero-subscriber result is not intrinsically a publication failure;
there may be no active interested node. It is not proof of absence of
interested observers either. Acquisition and recovery must handle that
distinction.

If a deployment elects both direct local dispatch and broker dispatch,
define which acceptance(s) discharge the publication intent. A local
fast-path receipt must not prematurely retire a required broker
publication. Partial failure across expanded scopes must be retryable
without creating additional business mutations or losing progression.
Initially routing the publishing node through its own broker
subscription may be simpler than optimizing the local fast path.

Origin-node identity is useful for diagnostics, not sufficient evidence
that local dispatch succeeded. Do not suppress an echo merely because
the origin is local unless the corresponding local obligation is already
satisfied or safely reconciled. Use explicit dispatch identity and
generation-aware state where deduplication is needed.

** Resolve source identity explicitly when distributing

The current outbox row and delivery path bind a source identifier,
including an explicit expected-source check. This is an important
qualification boundary, not an obstacle to remove.

In the multi-node profile, determine how a stable logical publication
domain relates to ephemeral local source instances, worker identities,
deployment identity and authority. A durable intent must remain
discoverable and deliverable by another admitted host after the
originating process fails, without allowing one deployment to dispatch
another deployment's publication.

Review durable source identifiers, worker discovery/lease filtering,
publication manifests, acknowledgment provenance and installed-host
admission together. A process-local capability or object identity is
not a portable credential. Do not rewrite the single-node checks to
pretend they are globally valid; derive and qualify the distributed
binding when the new profile is implemented.

* Relevance to the work currently in progress

** The authoritative operation proof should transfer

Claim exclusivity must hold whether two commands are submitted from the
same JVM or different web nodes. The proof must rely on the actual
protected authoritative prestate, atomic transaction semantics, command
identity and admitted effect footprint, not on JVM-local serialization.

The present work on independent retained physical execution, successful
commitment qualification, stale abort and guarded recovery is directly
relevant. It helps establish that the physical provider implements the
declared abstract transition under interference.

At v2756, the selected genuine executor results are bounded
correspondence evidence. Abstract committed transition correspondence,
composed recovery trajectories, source-derived authenticated provenance,
installed-host correspondence and CLM-R2 discharge remain outstanding.
See [[file:claim-proof-obligation-ledger.org][the Claim proof obligation ledger]].
Continue closing these obligations before starting the new deployment.

A future distributed proof should reuse the application-level induction
argument with a different qualified host/provider composition. It
must establish that all admitted hosts use the same declared semantics,
that every relevant physical writer is covered, and that actual
executions refine the shared authoritative transition relation.

Do not infer deployment-wide writer coverage from one process being
correctly installed. Mixed-version hosts, migrations, maintenance
writers, direct database access and worker processes can invalidate
that premise. Rolling deployment compatibility is a future explicit
qualification requirement, not a guarantee of the current profile.

** Present code and forward-compatibility decisions

| Area | Keep now | Qualify later |
|------+----------+---------------|
| Operation declarations and induction | Topology-independent guards, invariants, frames | All admitted host executions |
| Physical transaction refinement | Actual authoritative protected prestate and effects | Cross-host provider correspondence |
| Durable command identity | Stable authoritative incorporation and recovery | Retries arriving at another host |
| Invalidation expansion | Source-derived affected scope and progression | Hierarchical scope encoding and routing |
| Local Live source | Indexed matching and bounded consumer behavior | Broker adapter and local hierarchy |
| Outbox | Atomic intent, leases, fenced acknowledgment | Logical source domain and multi-host recovery |
| Progression | Opaque bases, compose without regression | Portable trusted basis and frontier across nodes |
| Acquisition | Qualified observation and owner installation | Upstream subscription epochs and gap recovery |
| Local caches and singleflight | Local optimization with authorization and basis checks | Cross-host equivalence and cache scope |
| Trust and evidence | Exact process-local authority boundaries | Deployment-wide installation and portable provenance |

Local locks, source objects, coalescers, caches, worker schedulers and
singleflight registries are perfectly legitimate when their promised
property is local. There is no benefit in replacing all of them with
distributed coordination now. The important distinction is whether
their locality is an optimization or an essential premise of the
business invariant or a claimed deployment-wide guarantee.

** Concrete rules for present development

- Do not embed Valkey, HAProxy, node affinity or a broker channel syntax
  in application business declarations.
- Do not make local observation or interest registration a prerequisite
  for deriving durable publication obligations.
- Preserve durable identities and actual transaction basis binding.
  Do not rely on message arrival order or origin process identity.
- Keep local dispatch acceptance, acquisition and installation separately
  named and qualified.
- Do not introduce JVM-local locks as substitutes for authoritative
  guards protecting shared business state.
- Keep provider-specific ordering and query consistency in the qualified
  authority adapter, not the generic transport.
- Keep permission checks and owner-generation checks on all relevant
  acquisition paths. Routing is not an authorization boundary.
- Keep process-local trust explicit. Avoid treating serialized data as
  evidence that an object-identity or trusted-executor premise holds.
- Preserve resource bounds, explicit failure, and recovery obligations.
  An overload path must not silently claim a refresh was installed.
- Do not broaden a current single-node admission claim merely to make
  an interface look future-proof. Add abstractions only where the
  present work actually benefits or an existing contract needs protection.

The existing architecture is largely aligned with these rules. The
identified source identity and adapter acknowledgment issues require
future extension, not immediate replacement. No current proof
obligation is retired by this design note.

* Performance and locality

HumanHelp's localized store workload is particularly suitable for
affinity-based routing. HAProxy may route a store's HTTP and SSE
connections to the same host to improve local dispatch, working-set
locality and safe fragment reuse. Affinity is not required for correctness.
A store may span several hosts, an admin may observe other stores,
and an acquisition may be served anywhere in the admitted deployment.

The proposed 20,000–50,000 SSE connections per node is a benchmark target.
The current local source defaults to 4,096 active consumers and bounds
pending puts per consumer. A consumer is not necessarily a browser socket.
Capacity requires empirical qualification of the entire stack: connection
and direct memory, TLS, proxy limits, scheduling, JVM GC, render cost,
XTDB reads, reconnect storms and authorization-sensitive cache hit rates.

The Valkey lookup selects interested web-node subscriber connections,
not all individual browser connections. This reduces inter-node fanout,
but broad invalidations necessarily incur real work. Local trie traversal
must enumerate the matching subtree and recipients. Do not make
unmeasured nanosecond latency or fixed capacity claims.

Use the existing leading/trailing coalescing, progression composition,
bounded pending work and fragment singleflight as appropriate. Add
bounded delivery scheduling, jitter and fair prioritization when
qualified measurements demonstrate a need. Jitter alone cannot cure a
sustained workload exceeding read capacity. Immediate actor settlement
and background observer refresh are separate latency paths.

Avoid needlessly broad root invalidations and high-cardinality channel
churn. A global configuration change may legitimately affect all hosts;
do not hide that necessary cost. Cache reuse must preserve authorization,
presentation parameters, authoritative basis and owner semantics.

Adding web nodes does not automatically shard the shared XTDB write
authority or increase its indexing throughput linearly. Database
partitioning would change the authoritative consistency and invariant
boundaries and is a separate design decision. Start with a shared logical
database and qualify its actual replication topology.

* Deferred implementation and acceptance sequence

This is a suggested future order, not a schedule or a change to the
present single-node critical path.

1. Establish the multi-node semantic contract, shared logical authority,
   deployment identities and qualified XTDB topology. Demonstrate
   equivalent operation behavior across two admitted hosts.
2. Implement a narrow exact-channel Valkey adapter using the existing
   scope and progression vocabulary. Use one broker initially, with
   explicit ephemeral semantics and qualified subscription establishment.
3. Integrate recoverable durable publication through that adapter,
   qualifying source/domain identity, lease handoff, receipts and partial
   dispatch failure. Do not add distributed per-client acknowledgments.
4. Implement transport-epoch acquisition and resync, authoritative
   frontier coverage and recovery of silent gaps. Demonstrate behavior
   when the SSE-serving host differs from the acquisition host.
5. Add segment-aware hierarchical scopes and prefix-expanded channel
   routing with desired/confirmed subscription reconciliation. Check
   semantic expansion independently of routing.
6. Qualify two-host and multi-host operation, recovery and observer
   behavior under faults and adversarial schedules. Bind the evidence to
   the installed deployment profile.
7. Benchmark representative local, overlapping and broad workloads.
   Introduce affinity, local fast path, channel sharding, more elaborate
   local indexes, or targeted history reconciliation only as justified.

Valkey persistence is not needed merely to maintain Pub/Sub subscriptions.
Valkey Streams, standalone routers and Rust SSE offload remain possible
optimizations or alternate delivery mechanisms if measured workloads
or a stronger requested recovery profile require them. They are not
the starting point. The semantic contract should not depend on them.

** Future regression and fault-injection matrix

| Scenario | Required outcome |
|----------+------------------|
| Two hosts submit competing Claim operations | Shared invariant preserved; correct distinct outcomes |
| Commit then publisher crash | Durable intent recoverable; no duplicate incorporation |
| Broker publish then worker crash before ack | Safe repeated publication under fenced recovery |
| Publisher accepts but subscriber misses message | Observer reacquires through qualified recovery |
| Broker disconnect while SSE remains open | Continuity marked uncertain; resync after restoration |
| First interest registers during a commit | No unaccounted gap across acquisition barrier |
| Last unsubscribe races with new interest | Desired subscription eventually confirmed; gap recovered |
| GET lands on a lagging different host | Wait for adequate basis or explicit failure |
| Notifications and responses arrive out of order | Pending requirement not weakened; installation not regressed |
| Ancestor invalidation and unrelated string prefix | Exact semantic segment matching |
| Child change affects an ancestor board | Expansion includes board; no inferred dependency shortcut |
| Multi-scope publication partly fails | Required effects recoverable; no false terminal acknowledgment |
| Origin echo and local fast-path failure | No unconditional suppression of needed refresh |
| Slow consumer or reconnect storm | Bounded resources, fair recovery, no false freshness claim |
| Authorization revoked during reacquisition | No unauthorized new observation or installation |
| Mixed deployment or incompatible basis | Reject or explicitly qualify compatibility, never silently accept |

Model checking can explore registration, dispatch, reconnection and
recovery schedules; mathematical correspondence can establish the
prefix transformation and progression composition properties.
Integration tests must exercise real Valkey and the qualified shared
XTDB deployment, not merely an in-process mock of their behavior.
The acceptance record must distinguish finite experiments, conditional
proofs, provider assumptions and fully established claims.

* Conclusion

The proposed prefix-expanded Valkey design is a promising simplification
of multi-node routing. Its strongest feature is that routing becomes
a relatively small transport concern, while the existing Gesso semantic
model continues to own authoritative operations, publication, acquisition
and progression.

The decisive observation is that failure to deliver an invalidation is
not fundamentally different from a client failing to come online and
read. Both leave an observer without an adequate authoritative
observation. The recovery obligations are real, but they do not demand
replay of every notification or a durable acknowledgment for every
browser.

We should complete single-node Gesso, preserve the boundaries described
above, and avoid topology-dependent business correctness assumptions.
When multi-node development begins, the goal is to qualify another
deployment of the same semantic system, not to invent another system.

No runtime implementation, dependency, proof status, admission profile
or canonical specification is changed by this note.
