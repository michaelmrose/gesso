#+SUBTITLE: Design investigation and reusable certification strategy
#+DATE: 2026-11-25

* Status and purpose

This non-normative design note explores how Gesso can support a broad
portfolio of applications whose important business rules are mechanically
checked. It supplements canonical Gessoplan R5.1; it neither changes that
specification nor introduces a runtime API, proof rule, admission policy,
certificate, or new completion criterion.

The implementation baseline is v2756, =03e5e689e3cf=, on =typed-clojure=.
The source observations below are grounded in that snapshot. Proposed
constructors, mathematical examples, and application portfolios describe
possible development, not features already available.

The objective is not to prove arbitrary Clojure programs, nor to require
application authors to become users of Typed Clojure, Ansatz, Recife, TLA+
or Quint. Authors describe their domain and Gesso constructions in ordinary
Clojure data. Gesso derives the executable semantic objects, obligations,
and, where supported, their mechanically checkable evidence.

The central hypothesis is that a relatively small vocabulary of composable,
well-specified operations can cover much of the correctness-critical behavior
of conventional database-backed applications. Generality should be measured
by the number of different business rules and application domains that can
reuse the same qualified constructions, not by the number of hardcoded
business operations in the framework.

* Why Claim is a representative proving problem

HumanHelp Claim is a useful vertical slice because it combines several
otherwise independent obligations:

- Trusted actor, command identity, and operation resolution.
- Eligibility depending on current authoritative Request, Membership and
  organizational policy.
- Exclusive ownership and the relationship between Request and assignment.
- Competing preparations and guarded atomic incorporation.
- Exact durable changes, evidence, journal and publication intents.
- Stale-attempt abort, guarded refusal recovery, and replay of an incorporated
  result without repeating the operation.
- Authoritative observation, invalidation, and eventual client settlement.

A scalar owner field alone does not prove the desired ownership theorem.
The invariant concerns the relevant active primary assignments, the admitted
lifecycle and the physical writers capable of modifying those facts. The
initial ownership epoch and subsequent legitimate release/reclaim behavior
must not be conflated.

The same structural problem occurs in reserving a seat, assigning a support
case, allocating equipment or appointing a responsible employee. Those
applications differ in their eligibility, lifecycle and effect declarations,
not necessarily in the mechanics of protected selection and atomic writes.

The reusable result of Claim should therefore be a set of source-derived
constructions and correspondence arguments, with a domain-specific instance
of their obligations. A second application should not need to reproduce the
entire Claim investigation merely to choose a different entity and guard.

As of v2756, independently reconstructed retained XTDB history can qualify
selected genuine executor commitments and stale mapped-prefix aborts.
Abstract committed transition refinement, recovery-trajectory composition,
authenticated premise provenance, complete installed-host correspondence
and CLM-R2 premise discharge remain open. These are important distinctions:
a successful selected diagnostic is not yet a theorem about all deployed
executions.

* The common mathematical model

** Typed guarded transitions

Let S be the well-typed declared state space, X_i the input space for
operation i, and T_i(s,x,s') its transition relation. For the guarded
replacement case, T_i holds only when the domains are satisfied, the guard
is true on the protected prestate and input, each assignment produces its
specified poststate value, and the frame preserves the presence and value
of every unwritten declared field.

#+begin_example
T_i(s,x,s') =
  well-typed(s,x,s')
  and G_i(s,x)
  and A_i(s,x,s')
  and F_i(s,s')
#+end_example

Assignments are simultaneous, not an implicit sequence of mutations.
The frame is relative to the declared projection; an actual transaction
must separately establish the complete relevant physical effect footprint.

This baseline is intentionally not the definition of every possible future
operation. Allocation, deletion, relational update, arithmetic, external
effects and resource transfer need their own explicit semantics. They may
share the transition interface without being disguised as scalar replacement.

** Simultaneous invariant preservation

For invariants I_1 ... I_n, define Inv(s) as their conjunction. For the
original declared initialization predicate and every admitted operation,
derive the following obligation cross-product:

#+begin_example
Initial(s)             => I_j(s)   for every j
Inv(s) and T_i(s,x,s') => I_j(s')  for every i,j
#+end_example

A proof of one invariant may depend on another invariant of the same
prestate. It need not preserve itself in isolation. If an invariant's
dependency footprint is disjoint from the actual changes, a sound frame
argument can often discharge that obligation automatically.

An initial predicate implying Inv is not evidence that an installed
database satisfies that predicate. A proof over the declared operation
catalog is not evidence that the catalog enumerates every deployed writer.
The inductive argument is applicable to real execution only when these
premises and the refinement relationship have been established.

Guards must not make an operation vacuously safe by disabling every
execution. Where an operation is intended to be usable, require reachable
or otherwise appropriately qualified positive witnesses, in addition to
preservation. Availability and progress require their own temporal claims.

** Physical and observational refinement

Let P be the relevant physical database state and alpha its declared
abstraction into S. For a qualified committed execution e with protected
prestate p and poststate p', the desired correspondence has the form:

#+begin_example
PhysicalCommit_i(p,x,e,p') =>
  T_i(alpha(p), x, alpha(p'))
#+end_example

This is schematic: identity, basis, trusted input, exact outcome and
physical effect witnesses must be bound to the same execution. The relation
must also account for durable command and publication facts where they are
within the certified subject.

An aborted attempt is not a committed transition. It requires an appropriate
no-durable-effects or permitted-effects argument for its scope. A retry
cannot retroactively make an earlier attempt a successful commitment.
A duplicate returning an existing result is not another incorporation.
A commit-unknown observation establishes neither rejection nor success.
Recovery and subsequent browser settlement have distinct obligations.

For a workflow, prove the relevant composition of those steps, including
interference and recovery, rather than treating a final database snapshot
as evidence that every intermediate transition was valid.

The closed scalar rule AST supports literal, read, presence, Boolean
connectives, equality, inequality, comparisons, membership and implication.
Its guarded replacement baseline assigns a literal or a required typed
prestate/input reader. It checks exact read/write declarations and frame
coverage within the declared projection. The syntax and constructor are
not themselves admission or proof.

The repository also contains opt-in signed-integer bound, nominal enum,
mixed induction, typed-read and mapped-prestate cardinality pilots. These
are substantial foundations, but their individual supported source shapes,
assumptions and proof scopes matter. For example, the reservation pilot
checks quantified propositions derived from an exact known catalog shape;
it does not establish general source-to-Ansatz translation soundness or
installed physical refinement. Integer ordering does not imply a general
arithmetic update language, and a mapped cardinality pilot does not imply
arbitrary relational aggregation is certified.

** Claim is not the entire framework

We should distinguish at least the following questions for any proposed
operation family:

- Can its intended behavior be expressed in the architecture?
- Is there an implemented closed construction for that behavior?
- Can Gesso derive and check the relevant abstract obligations?
- Is the derivation sound for that construction's supported domain?
- Are the actual installed writers, reads, effects and outcomes bound to
  those obligations?
- Does the evidence satisfy the requested application admission profile?

An affirmative answer at one level is not an affirmative answer at the
next. Application breadth must not be represented by invented coverage
percentages or by counting example tests as certified operation families.

* A reusable operation vocabulary

The following catalog is a proposal for organizing development. The first
families reuse the existing scalar and guarded-transition foundations;
others require additional semantic and physical qualification. None is
declared completely certified by appearing here.

** State and identity

- Lifecycle transitions: explicit allowed changes between nominal states;
  publish, archive, cancel, approve and reopen.
- Conditional replacement: change fields under typed prestate/input guards
  with an exact frame.
- Creation and deletion: fresh or deliberately reused identity, absence,
  existence, uniqueness, referential integrity, and lifecycle of references.
  Distinguish logical deletion from physical erasure and historical retention.
- Relationship updates: membership, ownership, assignment, parent/child
  associations, and changes to an explicitly scoped relation.
- Monotonic progression: version, epoch, generation and sequence changes.
  A new generation may permit an operation forbidden in the previous one.

** Quantities, sets and time

- Exact arithmetic: addition, subtraction, bounds and comparisons over
  integers or explicitly selected exact numeric domains.
- Fixed-point monetary quantities: declared currency and scale, rounding,
  overflow policy, conservation and explicit fee or adjustment accounts.
  Floating-point equivalence must not be presumed.
- Set and relation membership: insertion, removal, uniqueness, bounded
  cardinality, disjointness and scoped universal/existential predicates.
- Deterministic aggregation: sum, count and explicitly specified extrema
  over an authoritative selection, with correct empty-set semantics.
- Temporal intervals: overlap, containment, expiration, leases, ordered
  events and explicit endpoint conventions such as half-open intervals.
  Transaction time, valid time and application clocks are separate domains.

** Composition and effects

- Atomic compound operations: multiple writes with shared guards and one
  physical incorporation point; exact effect and frame boundaries.
- Command incorporation: idempotency, duplicate handling, identity
  compatibility, guarded abort/recovery and commit-unknown reconciliation.
- Multi-stage workflows: explicit durable intermediate states, per-stage
  authorization, retries, compensation and progress obligations.
- Authority-bearing actions: issue, use, transfer and retire capabilities
  under their actual scope and lifetime. Logical facts about a capability
  are not the capability and do not authorize its duplication.
- External effects: durable intent, dispatch, provider receipt, retry and
  reconciliation. No local proof alone establishes an external provider's
  behavior or exactly-once delivery.
- Read-side disclosure and presentation: authorized selection, permitted
  projections, scoped caches, invalidation interests, and correspondence
  of actual rendered fragments and client installation.

The aim is orthogonal primitives and a small number of construction families,
not a public DSL containing every conceivable business verb. Composition
must remain checked: two individually safe operations do not necessarily
form a safe workflow or an atomic operation.

* Qualification of a new primitive

A new semantic primitive should earn admission through the same discipline
as the existing guarded transition family.

1. Specify its domain, totality or partiality, equality, error semantics and
   resource limits. Reject unsupported inputs explicitly.
2. Give its typed source representation and its independent semantic
   meaning. Keep nominal identities and prestate/input/poststate distinct.
3. Specify its read dependencies, effects and frame, including relational
   and predicate dependencies where applicable.
4. Derive sound consequences and preservation obligations from the original
   source. Bind proof goals and evidence to that exact subject.
5. Establish source-to-executor and source-to-proof correspondence.
   Qualify the concrete XTDB lowering, protected prestate, concurrency,
   outcome and retained history on the supported provider profile.
6. Provide positive witnesses and adversarial mutations, including missing
   readers, false guards, wrong source, stale basis, interfering writers,
   incomplete frames and unsupported effects.
7. Record supported and unsupported cases and the remaining premises.
   Integrate into application preflight and the appropriate admission
   profile only when the relevant obligations are discharged.

For example, an absence or cardinality predicate is not protected merely
because a query returned the desired count. The physical transaction must
prevent an interfering insertion from invalidating the predicate between
preparation and incorporation, or correctly abort and recover. Actual XTDB
provider semantics and expression limits constrain the available lowering.
If a required primitive cannot be implemented soundly on the provider,
reject that construction rather than silently weakening the guarantee.

* Worked mathematical examples

These examples are proposed semantic specifications, not runnable Gesso
declarations or claims of current admission.

** Inventory reservation

Let stock and reserved be nonnegative integers associated with a specific
inventory identity. Require:

#+begin_example
I: 0 <= reserved <= stock
G: q > 0 and q <= stock - reserved
A: reserved' = reserved + q
F: stock' = stock
#+end_example

From I and G, the resulting reserved quantity is nonnegative and at most
stock. The proof is elementary arithmetic; the physical challenge is to
ensure competing reservations protect the same availability dependency.
Restocking, releasing reservations, shipping and stock corrections are
additional operations that must preserve the same invariant. A scalar
counter must also correspond to any detailed reservation records on which
the intended invariant depends.

** Transfer of a conserved quantity

For distinct accounts A and B in the same currency and a positive exact
amount q, a simple no-fee transfer requires:

#+begin_example
G: q > 0 and balance(A) >= q and A != B
A: balance'(A) = balance(A) - q
   balance'(B) = balance(B) + q
I: balances remain nonnegative
C: balance'(A) + balance'(B) = balance(A) + balance(B)
#+end_example

Conservation is a transition property (C); it can become a state invariant
relative to an initial total in a closed system. Deposits, withdrawals and
fees change the conservation boundary and require explicit accounting.
The two writes are simultaneous, and no partial incorporation is permitted.
Currency, precision, rounding and representation bounds are independent
obligations. A real ledger may additionally require immutable entries,
reversals, reconciliation and authorization rather than mutable balances
alone.

** Nonoverlapping bookings

For each resource r, define its active bookings as B(r). For distinct
bookings b and c, require:

#+begin_example
I: for every r and distinct b,c in B(r):
     end(b) <= start(c) or end(c) <= start(b)
G: start(new) < end(new)
   and for every b in B(r):
     end(b) <= start(new) or end(new) <= start(b)
A: B'(r) = B(r) union {new}
#+end_example

Half-open intervals allow adjacent bookings. The guard establishes every
new pair, while old pairs remain valid under the frame. Cancellation,
rescheduling and recurring reservations require additional constructors or
compositions. The physical absence predicate must be protected against
concurrent overlapping inserts, not only updates to existing bookings.
Time-zone conversion and ambiguous civil times must be resolved before
the authoritative interval comparison.

** Organizational authorization and disclosure

For a proposed operation on resource r, actor a must satisfy a declared
eligibility relationship with the authoritative organization owning r.
Membership status, role, delegation and resource ownership are potentially
mutable dependencies. A revocation racing with a write must be resolved
against the protected prestate according to the declared authority policy.

For reads, the property is different: returned information must belong to
the authorized projection at the specified basis and under the stated
disclosure policy. A write-side guard cannot by itself prove that a search,
SSE payload, error, cached fragment or rendered page discloses no
unauthorized information. If a policy claims noninterference across
executions, including observable differences, prove that stronger relational
property separately rather than confusing it with row filtering.

* Application portfolio and its likely reach

| Domain | Representative operations | Principal additional obligations |
|--------+---------------------------+----------------------------------|
| Administration and CRM | Assign cases, edit contacts, approve requests | Membership, ownership and lifecycle |
| HR and procurement | Approve leave, hire, allocate budgets | Policy, quotas, delegation, audit |
| Project and document management | Assign tasks, publish versions, archive | Dependencies, version/epoch and access |
| Scheduling and healthcare administration | Book appointments, reschedule, release | Interval exclusion and disclosure |
| Inventory and equipment rental | Reserve, allocate, return, replenish | Cardinality, quantities and availability |
| Commerce and marketplaces | Create orders, reserve stock, refund | Conservation, payment/provider contracts |
| Social and collaborative systems | Post, follow, moderate, message | Relationship uniqueness and read access |
| Finance and accounting | Transfer, reconcile, post journal entries | Exact arithmetic, conservation, audit |
| Scientific and algorithmic systems | Submit computation, approve results | Separate computation contracts |

This is a qualitative suitability assessment, not a claim that all these
applications can currently be certified or that Gesso satisfies the
regulatory requirements of any industry.

The strongest fit is software whose essential complexity is authoritative
state, permissions, consistency, coordination and lifecycle. Specialized
computations may remain outside the proven core, provided they cannot
bypass it. At the other extreme, a claim about numerical simulation
accuracy, optimal routing or cryptographic security requires distinct
domain-specific evidence. A web framework's transaction guarantees are
not a proof of those computations.

* A controlled boundary for ordinary application code

A marketplace may use ordinary Clojure for product search ranking,
recommendations, formatting, rendering and image processing. The certified
core can govern order creation, inventory reservation, seller authority,
payment-state changes, journal and publication.

This is not a license for arbitrary code to mutate certified state. An
ordinary algorithm can propose an input; the admitted operation must
validate it against current authority, the declared rule and its protected
prestate before committing. Opaque callbacks, direct writes and external
effects must either be constrained by a qualified interface or remain
explicitly outside the requested guarantee.

For an opaque transform, a checked pre/post contract may be sufficient if
the contract covers the relevant effects and the implementation is either
proved against it or constrained by a trusted enforcement boundary.
Testing a transform is not a substitute for a universal contract when
universal correctness is claimed.

The same distinction applies to UI behavior. Search results need not be
ranked optimally, but a certified disclosure policy must cover the actual
results. An optimistic presentation is not a durable success. Gesso
Choreo, Live, HTMX swaps and browser continuity have their own correspondence
obligations whenever included in the claim.

* Roles of the assurance tools

Typed Clojure is useful for making internal semantic categories, interfaces
and nominal identities difficult to confuse. It can reject particular
invalid compositions before execution. It does not, by itself, establish
business invariant preservation or that an observed database basis is
authentic.

Ansatz is a candidate for kernel-checked mathematical propositions, including
arithmetic, induction and more expressive relational properties. Proof
construction and checking must remain tied to the exact original Gesso
obligation, with an explicit trusted axiom/import policy and justified
translation. A checked theorem about a different model is insufficient.

Recife and other model-checking facilities can explore interference,
concurrency, recovery and temporal properties under their stated model and
bounds. Exhaustive finite checking is valuable but is not automatically
an unbounded theorem or a proof of the installed provider.

Gesso's executable semantic core remains the owner of application meaning.
The tools provide complementary evidence. The admission path must check
the relevant correspondence and residual premises, without silently
downgrading a requested guarantee or forcing developers to maintain multiple
manually synchronized specifications.

* Development strategy: a certification portfolio

** First close the existing vertical slice

Finish the outstanding Claim refinement, provenance and installed-host
obligations before treating that operation family as an established
end-to-end reference. Do not interpret this note as an instruction to
interrupt the current critical path or to start several new pilots at once.

The point of a subsequent portfolio is to test whether the existing
machinery transfers without reproducing every mechanism for each domain.
The examples should be small enough that the new semantic gap is apparent.

** Proposed sequence of transfer examples

| Example | Distinguishing obligation | Reuse target |
|---------+---------------------------+--------------|
| Lifecycle and publishing | Nominal transitions and version progression | Guard, frame, induction |
| Inventory reservation | Bounded exact quantity and concurrent allocation | Claim guards plus arithmetic |
| Transfer ledger | Conservation across atomic multi-entity writes | Compound effect correspondence |
| Appointment booking | Temporal exclusion and protected absence | Relational predicates and concurrency |
| Organization administration | Mutable, hierarchical authorization | Trusted input and policy dependencies |
| Order fulfillment | Multi-stage durable progress and compensation | Command recovery and publication |
| Private messaging | Authorized information disclosure | Read and presentation correspondence |

These are candidate miniature applications or proving fixtures, not seven
new production projects or mandatory additions to the canonical R5.1
completion scope. Existing suitable fixtures should be reused. Order may
change if the next genuine proof gap is elsewhere.

** What constitutes successful transfer?

Each pilot should declare its original source, invariant and operation
catalog, then exercise initialization, every interfering operation family,
source-derived proof generation, actual physical execution, and the
appropriate negative cases. Record unsupported paths and residual premises
under the requested profile.

A new example that needs only domain declarations and proof instantiation
is stronger evidence of generality than one requiring a new hardcoded
executor or proof translator. When the latter is unavoidable, identify the
missing general primitive, qualify it separately, and rerun prior examples.

The examples should form a permanent regression corpus. Deliberately
mutate guards, effect footprints, authorizations, source identities,
initialization, outcome records and physical lowering to ensure that
unsafe or unsupported constructions fail at the appropriate boundary.

* Measuring breadth without weakening assurance

Track coverage by semantic constructor, supported type and predicate
domain, proof obligation, source-to-execution correspondence, and admission
profile. Count independently qualified families separately from examples
and conditional diagnostics.

Useful measures include:

- Number of distinct application subjects reusing an unchanged qualified
  construction and physical lowering.
- Which invariant classes have sound, source-derived proof support.
- Which operations need handwritten evidence or still carry assumptions.
- Whether the complete initialization and operation-by-invariant obligation
  matrix is covered, including intervening or external writers.
- Whether adversarial cases expose unsupported effects and missing
  dependencies rather than being accidentally accepted.
- Whether the installed application satisfies the precise requested
  profile, as distinct from an isolated abstract proof.

No percentage of all possible applications can be inferred from these
measures. They nevertheless give us evidence that the marginal cost of
certifying new domains is falling.

* Conclusion

Gesso's opportunity is to exploit the structure of real applications:
typed domain state, guarded operations, explicit authority, atomic durable
effects, recoverable workflows and declared presentation. A relatively
small set of well-qualified semantic primitives should cover a considerable
portion of conventional business software.

The framework should allow ordinary computation wherever the declared
certification boundary permits it, while refusing unsupported or unproved
claims at that boundary. Generality is achieved by broadening the common
semantic vocabulary and proving its composition, not by weakening the
meaning of certification.

The decisive next evidence is successful transfer: a new domain declaring
its own rules, obtaining the necessary obligations from Gesso, and reusing
the previously qualified machinery through actual execution.
