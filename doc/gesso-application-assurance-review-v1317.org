* Disposition and scope

This v1317 deliverable is the *measured acceptance and open-assumption
review* specified in the v1268–v1317 roadmap. It is documentation, not a
new certificate, proof theorem, test runner, deployment hook, or change to
authoritative transaction behavior. The v1317 acceptance decision is
*bounded/source-relative checkpoint accepted; production certification NOT
established*. Conditional induction, independently sampled finite semantics,
and observed XTDB behavior remain distinct assurance classes.

The reviewed source baseline is the assembled *in-session source overlay
through v1316* rooted in the user's v1278 checkout at Git HEAD
=17a1856c89201859ef2868f149f2edf44e918bf5=. The subsequent files in
this overlay are independent revision artifacts, *not commits represented
by that HEAD*. Installing their corresponding =ins= archives in the user's
real checkout is a separate action. The source fingerprints below refer to
this in-session overlay; they are not signatures, remote attestation, nor
proof that the user's deployed tree matches it.

This review is confined to the Gesso-local declared Request/Claim pilot,
its source-bound induction/application machinery, and finite-state tests.
The HumanHelp production application is not certified. The R4 canonical
design is an *end-state contract*, not a description of completed proofs.
Formal liveness, deadlines, cancellation fairness, distributed-node
invariants and browser/CLJS/HTMX/SSE implementation refinement are outside
this checkpoint.

are in the declared machine remains external.

A correct isolated physical Claim update can match the declared step. A

successful committed witness. Tested competition produced a single

*test evidence*, not a universal ownership, authorization, linearizability,
or multi-node theorem. The Claim pilot's abstract invariant is deliberately
narrower than production Claim ownership uniqueness.

The observation data are copyable. A well-formed fabricated witness may
produce a positive diagnostic comparison. Consequently, the source says

=:universal-xtdb-refinement :not-established=. No digest or source recheck

** Independent finite oracle — v1308 through v1312

Sources:
- =doc/gesso-assurance-rule-soundness-boundary.org= (v1308): nine
  independent proof/implementation obligations S1–S9.
- =src/gesso/assurance/proof/finite_model_oracle.clj= (v1309): a separate
  data-only evaluator. It does not invoke Gesso's induction, rule, proof
  kernel or certificate checkers to decide its finite judgments.

Acceptance evidence:
- =test/gesso/assurance/proof/finite_model_oracle_test.clj=
  (v1310): hand-checkable Boolean, enum, integer, optional and malformed
  finite models; deterministic base and step counterexamples.
- =test/gesso/assurance/proof/induction_crosscheck_test.clj=
  (v1311): same original catalog sent to both implementations, including
  a four-operation finite lifecycle and guard/replacement mutations.
- =test/gesso/assurance/proof/induction_forgery_test.clj= (v1312):
  adversarial proof-source/authority tests.

The supplied finite universe is explicit: chosen values for each state
field and operation input, a bounded Cartesian product, and caps on
expressions, fields, operations, invariants and total cases. Boolean/enum
completeness is independently reported, while integer/keyword/string
representatives are *not* entire mathematical types. Successful finite
enumeration reports =:bounded-no-counterexample=, never a universal proof.
Unsupported syntax, undefined partial reads, empty initial populations,
truncated cases and budget exhaustion do not count as a successful result.
A concrete counterexample can falsify a claimed universal inference.
Conversely, a symbolic obligation remaining open while bounded
enumeration finds no counterexample can be conservative incompleteness.

Independence is at the level of a second *implemented evaluator* on the
restricted fragment. Its correctness and its relationship to an
independent mathematical semantics are not established metatheorems.

* Explicit open-assumption ledger

This is the decision-critical part of v1317. *OPEN* means no universal
proof/discharge has been provided; a passing regression or in-process
certificate does not close it. The S1–S9 identifiers are defined in
=doc/gesso-assurance-rule-soundness-boundary.org= (v1308).

** S1 — Typed source-language semantics: OPEN

Claim required: for every admissible typed expression, Gesso's concrete
rule evaluator agrees with a separately specified partial-map semantics,
including undefinedness and optional presence. v1309–v1311 add a separate
bounded evaluator and counterexamples, but do not cover all values or prove
its own semantics. Needed: external semantics specification, total rule
inventory and checked correspondence across every supported form.

** S2 — Propositional kernel rule soundness: OPEN

Claim required: every admitted kernel inference preserves semantic
entailment under precisely its reported assumptions. Kernel source tests
and substitution resistance are not formal semantics-preservation proofs.
Needed: individual rule lemmas (including premise handling, references,
normalization and nonclassical partial values) checked independently.

** S3 — Initial-consequence validity and real initializer coverage: OPEN

Claim required: base rule =Init(s) => I(s)= is sound, there are relevant
initial states, and *every actual initializer* starts within them. Source
checks do not enumerate production creation/seed/migration paths. Needed:
nonvacuity proof plus per-initializer implementation correspondence.

** S4 — Preservation-family soundness: OPEN

Claim required: all frame, literal, mixed and same-origin copy families
preserve the *whole invariant* under the declared transition semantics.
Exact frame including optional-field presence and guard definedness needs
independent justification. v1311 finite mutations add counterexample
coverage but are not an exhaustive proof over unbounded types. Needed:
family-specific rule theorems and a mechanically or independently checked
implementation correspondence argument.

** S5 — Induction composition and declared operation closure: OPEN

Claim required: accepted base plus all per-operation preservation leads to
invariants on every declared finite trace, and source/certification logic
cannot omit a declared invariant or operation. v1287 exercises this chain,
subject to S1–S4. Needed: independent composition theorem and explicit
trust boundary of the abstract operation catalog. External writers are
S7/S8, not discharged here.

** S6 — Live admission provenance and dependencies: OPEN

Claim required: certificate and dependency handles arise from fresh
checking of the intended original catalog/graph in a trusted runtime.
v1280–v1286 and v1312 check strong in-process identity and substitution
cases, but assume trusted runtime, registry and checker implementations.
Needed: defined trusted computing base, lifecycle and classloader threat
model, and independently justified issuance/verification semantics.

** S7 — Installed application and writer-set completeness: OPEN

Claim required: every deployed state-changing entry point (HTTP, job,
maintenance, migration, retry, direct XTDB write) is checked and remains
bound to its issued executor. v1292/v1294 inspect only a host-provided
snapshot and disclose unclassified entries. Strict policy does not by
itself install this inventory. Needed: enforced registration/inventory
at deployment, non-route writer audit, post-check binding stability,
production-app integration test, and an explicit app-level composition proof.

** S8 — Physical XTDB refinement and authorization: OPEN

Claim required: each real authorized physical commit refines some declared
abstract step with a trustworthy prestate, input/principal/command ID,
atomicity and full relevant frame coverage. v1298–v1307 provide controlled,
copyable, observed single-commit witnesses; v1300/v1302 compare those with
source-bound abstract plans. Neither a successful comparison nor a one-
winner contention test proves provenance or the all-transition property.
Needed: trusted execution-bound trace capture, physical writer closure,
real prestate/basis link, authorization dependency proof and actual XTDB
commit/refinement correspondence.

** S9 — Independent checker/metatheoretic soundness: OPEN

Claim required: a small independent semantic model plus proof that all
accepted checker rules are sound in it. A separately coded finite oracle
is helpful error detection but not a proof of the oracle nor of all Gesso
proof rules. Needed: a precisely stated logic/semantics, independent rule
checks (potentially TLA+/Recife/Quint or a proof assistant as generated
internal artifacts), and an explicit small trusted checker base. App
users should still only declare Gesso rules, not author theorem scripts.

** Additional R4 obligations outside the S1–S9 safety core

- Browser/CLJS/HTMX/SSE implementation refinement and continuity: OPEN.
- Liveness, timed timeout properties, fairness and scheduling: OPEN.
- Multi-node XTDB/Redis coordination and cross-node convergence: OPEN.
- HumanHelp production authorization, Claim owner uniqueness, production
  rules and deployed initializer/writer closure: OPEN.
- Durable observability, incident response and production evidence lineage:

These are distinct from failed tests: they are unmet *proof obligations*.
A passing bounded checkpoint cannot reclassify them as discharged.

* Recommended admission and release rules

1. *Allowed current description*: "Source-relative conditional abstract
   induction with same-process admission, a checked supplied application
   snapshot, bounded observed XTDB diagnostics, and independently evaluated
   finite representative models."
2. *Allowed application policy*: optional strict startup policy when the
   host explicitly invokes =require-inductive-policy!= against its current
   original sources. The result is snapshot eligibility only.
3. *Disallowed description*: "Independently proved Gesso soundness",
   "production application certified", "all XTDB transitions refine the
   specification", "all writers/authorization dependencies covered", or
   "diagnostic report provides transaction authority".
4. *No implicit fallback*: unsupported guarantees, unclassified writers
   when configured as mandatory, stale handles, failed source rechecks,
   aborted observations, and counterexamples remain blocked/open.
5. *No automatic enforcement claim*: installing v1315's namespace alone
   does not attach the gate to a production startup/router boundary.

* Final assessment

The v1268–v1317 roadmap has produced a meaningful layered assurance
system. It now has tests that can *find* incorrect declared transitions,
changed source/handler bindings, incomplete observed inventories, wrong
persisted XTDB writes, and differences exposed by an independently coded
finite evaluator. The strict application gate can reject a *supplied*
source snapshot that lacks the supported guarantees. Those are substantive
engineering achievements.

The remaining gap is not another status field or another "passed" report.
It is an independent semantic proof for the rule checker, plus a trusted
link from checked abstract operations to *all* actual deployed physical
executions. Until both exist, the release status remains:
